 Discussion Summary

>## How we used the LLM as a development partner

LLM used:Claude (Anthropic), in a chat conversation.

Prompt 1: We pasted the Lab 2 brief (AI in Everyday Challenges, Group 1 – The Forgetful Student) and asked the LLM to help us with the activity as Group 1. The brief required a product concept, name, key features, target user and a visual prototype.

Response: The LLM proposed - CarryOn, a mobile app with smart tags attached to students' essentials. Its main idea was to use an LLM to turn a student's timetable and deadlines into a personalised daily "what to bring" checklist. It listed six features: the smart checklist, tag-based item detection, left-behind alerts, an "Ask CarryOn" assistant, context-aware extras (weather, deadlines) and a pre-exit check. It also produced a three-screen app mock-up (Today checklist, Left-behind alert, Ask CarryOn).

Follow-up prompts:We asked the LLM how to document the work in our GitHub portfolio, and how to divide the work between the two of us.

Ideas we kept: The CarryOn name and concept, the timetable-driven checklist, left-behind alerts and the mock-up.

Ideas we changed or rejected: We kept the concept as proposed

## Group discussion points

Why the concept fits the problem: Students' needs change with their timetable, so a checklist that knows the day's lectures, labs and deadlines is more useful than a generic reminder.

Trade-offs and risks the LLM raised: privacy of timetable and location data, the cost and battery life of tags, and the accuracy of the LLM's suggestions, which means students must be able to edit the list.

Division of work: Member 1 led the prompting and concept development; Member 2 reviewed the mock-up and wrote the documentation (see 'group-members.md').

## Reflection on using LLMs for problem solving

What the LLM did well: It turned a short brief into a complete, structured concept (name, user, features, prototype) very quickly, and it connected the LLM to the product itself rather than only using it as a brainstorming tool.

Limitations: The concept is a design only. We have not tested whether students would use it, and the LLM's feasibility points (tag cost, battery, privacy) would need real research. The mock-up is a static drawing, not a working app.

Effect on our thinking: We had to evaluate the LLM's proposal critically instead of accepting it, check it against the brief's requirements, and decide what to keep, which exercised our critical thinking more than starting from a blank page would.
